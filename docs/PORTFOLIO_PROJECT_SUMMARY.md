# Lagos Formal Public-Transport Accessibility

## Mapping access to Lagos's formal transit network

Public transport accessibility is not only about where transit routes
exist. It is also about whether people can realistically reach them.

I analysed walking access to mapped formal/core public-transport points
across Lagos State using a pedestrian network and spatial population data.

The analysis found that approximately **6.21 million people (53.44%)** are
within 30 minutes' walk of mapped formal/core transit, while approximately
**5.41 million (46.56%)** are beyond 30 minutes or fall within a structural
walking-network gap under the reference model.

Accessibility improves as the threshold expands: **22.02%** of the
population is within 15 minutes, **71.90%** within 45 minutes and **79.49%**
within 60 minutes.

The analysis also tested the 500 m population-to-network connection
assumption against 250 m, 750 m and 1,000 m alternatives.

A major limitation is important for interpreting these results: Lagos
depends heavily on informal mobility, particularly danfo minibuses, okada
and keke. These services are not comprehensively represented in the mapped
transit dataset.

The project therefore maps **formal-transit accessibility gaps**, not the
absence of transport itself.
